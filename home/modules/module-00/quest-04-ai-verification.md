## Your Mission

AI can give you an answer that sounds polished, specific, and completely confident.

That doesn't make the answer true.

As an analyst, your job isn't simply to get an answer. Your job is to know **what evidence supports it**.

**Target:** Distinguish a Verified Fact from an Unverified AI Claim and choose a concrete way to verify information before using it.

---

## Vocabulary Mission

By the end of this quest, you should be able to recognize, use, and explain:

* **AI-Generated Claim**
* **Verified Fact**
* **Evidence**
* **Source**
* **Verification**
* **Cross-Check**

Keep this workflow in mind:

**Claim → Check → Evidence → Trust**

# ⚡ Checkpoint: Trust It or Check It?

An AI assistant tells you:

> “There are 12,418 rows in this dataset.”

You have the dataset open in your analysis environment.

What's the strongest next move?

**A.** Use the number because AI gave a precise answer.

**B.** Ask the AI whether it's sure.

**C.** Check the dataset yourself.

Lock in your answer before opening help.

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint — Open After Your First Attempt</summary>

Getting the AI to repeat the claim isn't the same thing as finding **independent evidence**.

What could you inspect directly?

</details>

Try again before revealing the Answer.

<details>
<summary>✅ Answer — Open After Your Second Attempt</summary>

**C. Check the dataset yourself.**

If you have access to the dataset, you can directly verify the number of rows rather than relying on the AI's claim.

Asking the AI whether it's sure only gives you another AI response. It does not independently verify the original claim.

**Confidence is not evidence.**

</details>

## 🎮 Debug the Candidate Answer

A candidate says:

> **“If ChatGPT gives me a detailed explanation and the code runs, I know the answer is correct.”**

### Your Challenge

What's wrong with this reasoning?

Identify **one concrete action** you could take to verify the AI-generated answer.

Make your first attempt before opening the Hint.

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint — Open After Your First Attempt</summary>

Two things can be true at the same time:

* Code can **run successfully**.
* The result can still be **wrong, misleading, or inappropriate for the task**.

What independent evidence could you use to check the result?

</details>

Try again before revealing the Answer.

<details>
<summary>✅ Answer — Open After Your Second Attempt</summary>

A detailed explanation and code that runs are **not proof that the answer is correct**.

Running successfully only tells you that the code executed. It does not prove that the logic, assumptions, data interpretation, or result is correct.

A concrete verification step could include:

* Inspecting the original data.
* Testing the result with a known example.
* Checking the calculation yourself.
* Comparing the claim against a trustworthy source.

The professional habit is:

**Claim → Check → Evidence → Trust**

</details>

## 🛡️ Workflow Unlocked

Before relying on an AI-generated claim:

### Claim → Check → Evidence → Trust

**Claim:** What exactly is the AI telling me?

**Check:** How could I test or verify it?

**Evidence:** What reliable information supports the claim?

**Trust:** Do I now have enough evidence to use it?

The goal isn't:

**Never Use AI.**

The goal is:

**Know What You've Verified.**

# 💼 Interview Arena

**One Question. One Strong Answer.**

An interviewer asks:

Think of **one concrete verification method** before revealing help.

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint — Open If You're Stuck</summary>

Don't answer only with:

**“I double-check it.”**

Explain **how** you would double-check it.

What independent evidence could you inspect?

</details>

<details>
<summary>✅ Example Answer — Open After You've Answered</summary>

I don't treat an AI response as verified just because it sounds confident.

I identify the specific claim and then check it against independent evidence—for example, inspecting the original dataset, testing the result myself, or checking a trustworthy source.

I only rely on the claim once I have evidence that supports it.

</details>
